We talk a lot about great leadership—how the best managers lift people up, create space for growth, and show up when it matters. But not every leadership story is inspiring.  

**Some are cautionary tales.**  

I've experienced it myself in the past. Maybe you have, too.  

There's a particular kind of manager who seems to climb the ladder faster than most. Charismatic. Strategic. Often politically savvy. But behind the scenes? A different picture unfolds. One where the team is left unsupported, trust is quietly eroded, and fear spreads faster than feedback.  

**Let's call them what they are: The Selfish Manager.**  

*(Quick disclaimer: This isn't about my current managers—you all rock! But if any past leaders are reading this... well, no hard feelings. Maybe a little self-reflection? 😉)*  

## 🚩 Traits of a Selfish Manager  

### 🎯 Zero Accountability  
Mistakes? That's your problem. Wins? They'll gladly take credit. When things go wrong, they're already pointing fingers—or ghosting the conversation entirely.  

### 🚫 No Support When It Counts  
When pressure hits, they disappear. Not because they don't see the struggle, but because helping you doesn't serve them.  

### 🙌 Credit Taker, Blame Dodger  
They'll smile at the team lunch and throw you under the bus in the exec meeting. Everything is transactional - help is only given if there's a return.  

### 💼 Transactional to the Core  
If it doesn't serve their goals, it's not their concern. Help and support are conditional.  

### 🔥 Operates in Survival Mode  
They'll sacrifice trust, morale, and long-term success just to keep their image intact.  

## 💥 The Fallout  

- **Low Morale & High Turnover** – People don't leave companies, they leave managers.  
- **Fear-Based Culture** – Teams stay quiet to avoid being thrown under the bus.  
- **No Growth** – Without mentorship or advocacy, employees stagnate.  
- **Broken Trust** – Once trust is gone, it's hard to rebuild.  

## How to Survive  

There's no one-size-fits-all fix, but here are some strategies I've seen work—either for myself or others:  

- **Keep Records.** When you're unsupported, documentation becomes your safety net.  
- **Find Your People.** Allies, mentors, or even just someone to vent to—they matter more than you think.  
- **Set Limits.** Don't overextend for someone who wouldn't do the same for you.  
- **Know When to Leave.** Sometimes, leaving isn't giving up—it's reclaiming your career.  

## 💬 Final Thoughts  

Here's what I've come to believe: **bad managers reveal more about themselves than they do about you.** Your struggle under them isn't proof of your weakness - it's a symptom of their failure to lead.  

If you've worked with someone like this, you're not alone. And you're not powerless either.
